# ISOC Youth Ambassadors 2027: draft answers

- **Window:** not announced. Last year it was 18 Nov – 2 Dec 2025, only two weeks.
- **Eligibility:** age **18–30 on the application date**, strong English, at least 4 hours a week for 12 months, weekly calls between 11:00 and 16:00 UTC (20:00–01:00 in Tokyo), and a valid passport.
- **Selection:** application review, then an online Internet-governance course. The 15 best performers are chosen.
- **What you get:** a funded trip to a major Internet governance event matched to you. The exact coverage isn't listed, so confirm it when the call opens.
- **Fee:** none

> ⚠️ **Blocker:** I don't know your date of birth. If you'll be over 30 when the call opens, skip this program.
> ⚠️ The weekly calls fall late in the evening Tokyo time. Check that works alongside your job.

## Why do you want to be a Youth Ambassador? (~180 words)

I'm a software engineer, and most of my work sits one layer above the Internet's plumbing: cloud services, APIs, caching, dashboards. I know how to build on the Internet. I know much less about who decides how it works, and I've started to feel that gap.

Living in Japan as an Indian engineer has made that concrete. I've seen how much depends on language and access. The same service can be easy for one person and unusable for another because of the script it uses, where they're connecting from, or whether they can read the error message. Those aren't only engineering problems. They come from decisions made in policy spaces that young engineers rarely enter.

I want to be the engineer in the room who can explain what a policy means in practice, and then take what I learn back to other engineers. I also want to hear first-hand how young people in other regions experience the Internet. Their constraints are different from mine in Tokyo, and I would learn more from them than from any course.

## What perspective would you bring? (~120 words)

A practical, cross-border one. I have six years of hands-on engineering: backend services, cloud infrastructure on AWS and Azure, and real-time dashboards, for an international airline, an automotive R&D centre and Japanese industrial clients. I speak English, Hindi, Marathi, Kannada and Telugu, plus intermediate Japanese. That lets me connect with communities in South Asia and in Japan. I also represent a large and fast-growing group, foreign tech workers in Japan, whose view is rarely heard in Internet governance.

## How will you use the program? (~100 words)

- Write one practical explainer each quarter, in English and Japanese, that turns an Internet-policy topic into an engineer's checklist. Examples: universal acceptance, and securing IoT devices at home.
- Give a talk at an engineering meetup in Tokyo about how developers can take part in Internet governance.
- Represent young technologists from Asia-Pacific honestly at the matched event, and report back to the cohort.
